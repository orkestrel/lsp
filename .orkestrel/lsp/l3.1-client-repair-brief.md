### Item 1 — lifecycle state machine (verdict claim 3)

Defects, all in `src/core/LSPClient.ts`:

- Concurrent `start()` double-handshakes: the analyst executed racing calls and observed
  `initialize#1, initialize#2, initialized, initialized`. Cause: no shared in-flight start state
  at `:130-168`.
- `close()` is entirely ungated at `:193-210`: callable before `start`, during the handshake, and
  after `destroy`, writing `didClose` into whatever state holds.
- A hung `shutdown` draws `$/cancelRequest` after `shutdown` via the generic deadline path at
  `:455-470` — the protocol permits only `exit` after `shutdown`. The analyst executed
  `shutdown#2, $/cancelRequest, exit`.
- A deadline on `initialize` itself cancels pre-handshake: nothing but the handshake may precede
  the initialize result, and `$/cancelRequest` before the result is forbidden.
- A transport exit does not gate later writes: `destroy()` after a peer exit still writes `exit`
  into the dead generation (analyst, executed).

Ruling: the client owns a lifecycle state machine. One state answers every door — what `start`,
`open`, `close`, `destroy`, a write, and a cancel notification may do is read from the state,
never from a scattered field. Only `exit` follows `shutdown`; nothing but the handshake precedes
the initialize result; a dead generation refuses writes with `LSPError` code `'closed'`.
Concurrent `start()` shares the in-flight promise. If the state is a real domain mode, represent
it as a literal union in `src/core/types.ts` per the design laws (named axis, no decorative
labels, derive what is derivable); keep it private unless a consumer needs it.

Ruling: `start()` after a failed handshake or a peer exit begins a fresh transport generation —
the client calls `transport.start()` again. Item 4 documents the paired obligation on the
interface.

### Item 2 — push deadline and session-scoped diagnostics (verdict claim 4)

Defects:

- The push path has no deadline: with a small `timeout`, a peer that accepts `didOpen` and never
  publishes leaves `open()` pending forever (analyst, executed). Bound push settlement with the
  same deadline discipline the pull path has, and route its cleanup through shared logic.
- A full report that omits `resultId` leaves the prior id in play at `:264`: the analyst executed
  full `A` with `result-a`, then full `B` without a `resultId`, then `unchanged/result-a`
  returned `A`. A full report without `resultId` deletes the prior entry.
- `#diagnostics` (declared `:83`, used `:239`, `:258`, `:266`) survives exits and teardowns: no
  clear site exists. Ruling: session state lives and dies with the session — clear
  `#diagnostics` wherever `#capabilities` clears (`#receiveExit`, `#teardown`).

### Item 3 — one settlement path for send faults (verdict claim 5)

Defect: a transport whose `send` throws synchronously escapes as a raw `Error` and leaks its
pending entry — `#request` registers the pending before calling `send()` with no synchronous
exception boundary at `:296` (analyst, executed on the diagnostic request; the same shape sits in
`#write`). Route synchronous throws and asynchronous rejections through the one settlement path,
remove the pending entry, and reject coded `'closed'`.

Folded note from the reviewer: a JSON-RPC error response carrying `id: null` — the server-side
parse-error report — surfaces as generic `'protocol'` instead of `'server'` with its wire code,
and the `id === null` guards at `:390` and `:401` are unreachable dead paths. Surface the null-id
error response as `'server'` carrying the wire code, and remove the dead guards.

### Item 4 — teardown totality and the transport seam (verdict claim 6, findings B and C)

Defects:

- The `exit` write at `:516` is unbounded when `send` never settles. Bound it.
- A transport `close()` that throws synchronously at `:518` breaks `destroy()`: the rejection
  escapes the destruction promise, listeners stay attached, the emitter survives (analyst,
  executed). Normalize a synchronous close throw into the same handling as a rejection, and put
  listener removal, state clearing, and emitter destruction in `finally`.
- A `close()` rejection arriving after the destroy deadline reports to nobody: `:520` emits
  `error` after `:534` destroyed the emitter. Ruling: the failure is surfaced before the emitter
  dies, and the ordering is pinned by a test.
- `LSPTransportInterface` (`src/core/types.ts:246-249`) documents no obligations. Ruling — put
  these in its TSDoc: `send` and `close` reject rather than throw; `send` after `close` resolves
  `false`; `start` may be invoked again only after `close` resolved or `exit` fired, and an
  implementation that cannot reconnect rejects that call. The client still defends both
  directions, because a foreign implementation is exactly what the seam admits.

### Item 5 — owned retained bytes and the malformed parameter (verdict claims 1 and 2)

Defects, both in `src/core/parsers.ts`:

- Retained bytes are not owned. The state aliases the caller's mutable chunk at `:40` — the
  analyst mutated a delivered buffer after the call and corrupted the continuation — and the
  remainder at `:276` is a `subarray` that pins the whole frame buffer behind a one-byte tail.
  One rule closes both: retained bytes are owned copies. Copy on retention and `slice` the
  remainder; the state the parser returns is safe against caller mutation and pins no delivered
  frame.
- A `Content-Type` parameter without `=` is silently skipped at `:185`:
  `application/vscode-jsonrpc; charset` decodes instead of refusing (analyst, executed). Refuse a
  malformed parameter in a known header field while keeping unknown header fields tolerated.
